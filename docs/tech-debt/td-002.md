# TD-002 – Disabling a Client does not revoke its Refresh Tokens

- **Status:** Open
- **Added:** 2026-10-11
- **Area:** Client Management (`Client.Disable`, `DisableClientService`)

## Current state

- `POST /api/clients/{id}/disable` only sets `Client.Status` to `Disabled` and logs the change.
- Refresh Tokens that were already issued to the Client are not touched.

## Why

Refresh Tokens do not exist yet. They are part of the OAuth runtime (`auth` schema), which is planned for a later phase. There is nothing to revoke until then.

## Impact

- Today: none, because no tokens are issued yet.
- Once Refresh Tokens exist: a disabled Client could keep exchanging its Refresh Tokens for new Access Tokens until they expire (`RefreshTokenTTL`), unless this entry is resolved. That would undermine US-014.

## How to resolve

When the Refresh Tokens are implemented:

1. Revoke all active Refresh Tokens of the Client in `DisableClientService`, in the same transaction as the status change, and log how many were revoked. The service already has a remark at the place where this belongs.
2. Also reject `grant_type=refresh_token` at `/token` when the Client is `Disabled`, as a second line of defense. This also covers tokens that are issued while the Client is being disabled.
3. Decide what happens to Refresh Tokens when the Client is enabled again. They should stay revoked, so the Client applications have to sign in again.

Access Tokens that were already issued stay valid until they expire (`AccessTokenTTL`), because they are checked without calling Authora. A short `AccessTokenTTL` limits this window.
